# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Buyers with money, on a short list.** Compliance and product heads at brokers, crypto venues and neobanks have budgets and can be listed by firm. Regulators starting to describe this need (MAS SAFR, FINRA) gives a reason to buy.
- **It goes after effort and time.** An MCP proxy with no code change on the broker's side, and evidence exported under the supervisor's own headings, cut the two costs that usually sink enterprise offers.
- **It does not compete on price.** $60k to $250k a year against regulatory exposure fits how compliance buys. The difference is checks built for trading and payments plus exam-ready evidence; the neighbours (Runlayer, Archestra) sell general guardrails.
- **Real scarcity:** five design-partner slots. The consumer app makes the product concrete in a demo.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The pain may not belong to the buyer yet.** If brokers can do what Robinhood did and put agent-trade risk on the customer, the compliance officer's pain stays small until a regulator requires something or an incident happens. No interviews are done. How many mid-size firms are opening accounts to agents, and what they would spend, are unknown. A weak market is where this lens most often passes.
2. **Perceived likelihood is close to zero.** Compliance buys certainty. Today: no customers, no references, no SOC 2, one founder, an identity standard (AINRA) the founder also started, and contracts that cap liability as "decision support". Gate also sits in the trade path and fails closed, so the vendor's outage becomes the buyer's.
3. **The offer is spread thin and has no guarantee.** Three products, five kinds of buyer, and a stack that is a feature list (adapters, webhooks, kill-switch API), not the buyer's obstacles solved one by one. Six free months with the price named afterwards tests nothing about willingness to pay, and leaves about six months to reach $480k ARR.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Under terms like Robinhood's, who at which kind of firm takes the blame when an agent trade goes wrong, and what does it cost them?
- How many firms in the first segment have opened accounts to agents, and what do they spend on compliance tools today? (Open question.)
- What proof can a compliance officer see in 30 days? How long from signature to an evidence export an examiner would accept?
- With liability capped, what will you guarantee?
- Why would a broker accept passports from a registry the founder started, before any other identity adapter exists?
- Who pays $1,500 a month for a million governed actions? Does a per-action meter follow the value the buyer gets?
- At double the Enterprise price, is Gate still the obvious choice over building in-house?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Discovery first.** The 20 interviews happen, and most broker and venue interviewees call this their firm's own problem, with a named budget owner.
2. **Charge for the pilot.** A short paid pilot, or an LOI with the price signed before it starts, with at least two partners.
3. **One buyer, one offer.** Gate for mid-size brokers and crypto venues opening MCP access to agents. The app stays a demo; Arena waits.
4. **A stack built on the buyer's path, with a guarantee.** A readiness assessment, ready-written policies and supervisory procedures, an exam-ready evidence pack, help through the buyer's vendor security review; for example, no fee if the evidence pack fails their internal audit review.
5. **A trust plan before selling.** A SOC 2 timeline, AINRA under separate governance, and at least one third-party identity adapter.
